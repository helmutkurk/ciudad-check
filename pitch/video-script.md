# Demo video script (target 3:00, hard limit 3:30)

Production (Saturday timeline, edit decision list, capture, fallbacks): [video-plan.md](video-plan.md). Challenge shots follow ADR-19 (hand sign, `screen_recapture` cheat).

The video is not shown in the live pitch; judges watch it on their own afterwards. It must stand alone: a judge who has never heard of Shelfie understands who it is for, the problem, how it works, the business case and one paid job from start to finish, with its transactions on a preprod explorer. Track requirement (prizes page): demo video of at most 2.5 minutes showing "who the project is for, the problem, the business case and one paid job or request from start to finish".

Spoken English, calm, one voice for the whole video: Gemini TTS in Google AI Studio, voice "Nika", one style prompt for all shots ("a young founder telling a friend about their project", generated Sat 31.10.; shot 6 is regenerated on Saturday with the sign that actually appeared). Every step carries a numbered on-screen title, so the flow is readable with the sound off; subtitles are burned in.

## Word count and timing

The read-through copy below has 404 words. At about 140 wpm that is about 2:15 of speech; with 0.7 s of air per shot the cut lands near 2:24. Shot 7 names the location check (engine, before the verifier; a deterrent, not proof, which the "Today and next" card says). Shot 10 says "the worker gets nothing", not "nothing paid": the verifier is paid for a fail too (ADR-18). Each shot lasts as long as its voice-over take (the cutter enforces it), so a slow read makes the video longer, not the speech faster. If the export runs over 2:25, trim in this order: shot 2 second sentence, shot 9 last clause, shot 12 first sentence. Recheck after any edit with `wc -w`. Upper bound: 320 words.

## Fallback cut if escrow is not live

Order of preference, decide before recording starts:

1. **Escrow ran earlier and succeeded:** use that run's recordings; caption "earlier run".
2. **Escrow works but the cycle is too slow (60+ min):** place the order first; record everything else while it runs; insert the settlement clip when it completes, else the earlier-run clip.
3. **Escrow not live at all** (the go/no-go in `../diseno/architecture.md` section 6): the job comes from the dashboard and is labelled "demo job, no escrow" on screen; the lead still pays the verifier over x402 and the person by plain preprod transfer. Shot 4 says "The brand orders one check in our app"; shot 9 shows the escrow lock and refund from earlier runs with the caption "earlier run". Never show a mocked transaction as real.
4. **Verifier unreliable:** fix light, distance or tag and re-record. The manual override (`../diseno/verifier-rules.md` section 8) is off for every recorded take.

## Voice-over (clean read-through copy)

Alex wants to bring a new grocery product to Germany. Before launching, he needs to know what similar products cost, and whether they're really on the shelf. Like most small brands, he has no field team here, and an agency store visit costs up to five hundred euros, even when the photo is blurry, old or from the wrong shop. An AI can't see the shelf. So Shelfie is an agent that hires people to take the photo, and pays only once a verifier agent confirms it's true. The brand orders one check, for this product in this store. The fee is locked in Masumi escrow on Cardano, so nobody is paid yet. Shelfie turns the order into a task for people nearby, and whoever claims it first gets the job. In the store, the camera opens, and only then shows a one-time hand sign: five fingers, left of the tag, ninety seconds. An old photo can't show a sign that didn't exist. The first photo was too blurry. The verifier said no, was still paid for its check, and the worker simply tried again with a new sign. Shelfie's agent checks the phone's location against the store, then hires a verifier agent and pays it per check over x402. It reads product and price, and checks the hand sign and the time. Every gate passes. Since the photo passed, the worker is paid at once, on Cardano preprod. Here's the transaction. The brand gets its report, and the escrow releases the fee. If no photo passes in time, the brand is refunded automatically, like in this earlier test run. Every step leaves a public receipt on Cardano: the escrow lock, both verifier fees over x402, and the worker's payout. What about cheating? Here the camera is pointed at a printed photo of the shelf, far from the store. No hand sign, wrong place: it's rejected, and the worker gets nothing. Market research is a hundred and fifty billion dollar business, built for big budgets: an agency checking a few hundred stores costs three hundred thousand dollars a year. Shelfie charges per verified photo instead. The brand pays twelve euros per check, a fraction of an agency visit. The worker earns three fifty, and failed checks cost nothing. Today: one shelf check on preprod, with stronger location proof next. The same engine fits any proof-of-real-world task. Shelfie: paid only when the photo is true.
